# User Journey

[← Back to README](../README.md)

This journey follows Noah, the international student from Bad Situation 1, through Thanksgiving week as he hosts a dinner with our app instead of eating alone. Noah is the host, so his guests appear only through what he sees on his table page.

**The trigger.** On the Monday before Thanksgiving, Noah's friends leave for the airport and the dining hall posts reduced holiday hours. Flying home to Singapore for four days is far too expensive, and he would rather share a Thanksgiving dinner than eat alone. The last time he tried, his post in the dorm list drew five replies of "maybe, depends where and how much," and the search for a restaurant that suited strangers' diets and budgets dragged on until everyone drifted off.

**1. Signing in.** Noah opens the app and signs in through institutional SSO (MIT Touchstone or Commons login; no email/OTP code is required). Because the app uses a Zoom-link model without a public table board, Noah goes straight to the **create table** screen to open a dinner and share it with his dorm and club channels.

**2. Creating the table.** On the **create table** screen, Noah titles the table "Thanksgiving dinner for anyone staying on campus," sets the time to 6 to 8 PM on Thursday, the area to Central Square, and five open seats besides his own. He sets the RSVP cutoff to Wednesday at 8 PM so he can call a restaurant before the holiday. The screen then asks how the venue will be chosen. A host who already has a restaurant or dining hall in mind can enter it right away, and even mark the table booked once they have made the reservation, so guests simply claim seats with no poll created. Noah has no restaurant in mind, so he leaves the restaurant unselected to let the invitees vote, seeding the poll with a Thai place and an Indian buffet he has walked past. He scopes seat claims to `mit.edu` and notes in the details that everyone pays for their own meal.

**3. Sharing the link.** On the **share** screen, Noah creates two labeled links, one for his dorm list and one for his cultural club chat. Dropping a link feels far less exposing than his old post, because the table page already carries the time, area, and live seat count. Nobody has to reply "still room?" in front of everyone, and there is no visible thread of maybes to watch go quiet.

**4. Seats filling.** Over Monday night and Tuesday, Noah watches the seat count on his **table page** drop from five to zero, and a sixth student who arrived late joins the waitlist. Each guest only had to open the link and click accept; any guest who was not yet signed in was sent through institutional SSO login and had their acceptance and seat reservation completed automatically upon return. On Tuesday evening, one guest gets a last-minute ticket home and withdraws, and the seat goes straight to the waitlisted student, who is notified that their seat is confirmed. Noah never has to post an update.

**5. Choosing where to eat.** From the table page, Noah opens the **tablemate profiles**, which only the host and seated guests can see. They show that one tablemate keeps halal, one is vegetarian, and most prefer a cheap meal within a short walk, without anyone announcing a restriction in a chat. A guest adds a Middle Eastern restaurant with halal and vegetarian options to the **venue poll**, and by Wednesday afternoon it has the most approvals.

**6. Confirming before the cutoff.** On Wednesday, seated guests get a reminder two hours before the cutoff to release their seat if their plans changed, and nobody does. An hour before the cutoff, Noah gets a reminder to confirm the table, which he does from the table page. At 8 PM the seats lock, the poll closes, and the Middle Eastern restaurant becomes the table's venue.

**7. Booking.** Noah calls the restaurant with an exact headcount of six, books a table for Thursday at 6 PM, and marks the table booked, so every guest sees the confirmed venue on the same page they used to join.

**The outcome.** On Thursday evening, Noah sits down in Central Square with five other students who stayed on campus, two of whom he had never spoken to. He never had to ask whether anyone was free, never negotiated a restaurant with strangers in a group chat, and never lost a seat to a stale message, since the withdrawn seat went to someone who still wanted it. Students on his dorm list who wanted a quiet break simply ignored the link and heard nothing further.
